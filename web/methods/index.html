Methods and sources

The unit of analysis is the 2020 Neighborhood Tabulation Area (NTA). Only residential NTAs (ntatype = '0', 197 areas) are shown; parks, cemeteries, airports and other non-residential areas are left out. All data was downloaded directly from the public sources below on 2026-09-26.

Heat Vulnerability Index

The HVI rank (1 = least vulnerable, 5 = most) is used as published by the city. The city builds it from these neighborhood inputs: surface temperature, green space, percent of households with air conditioning, median household income, and percent Black population. The file lists one NTA, BX0802 (Kingsbridge-Marble Hill), twice with identical values and different GEOCODEs; one copy is dropped. Percent Black population is used only for one adjustment in the statistics below (HVI rank vs frequent mental distress, controlling for median income and percent Black population). It is not mapped, and it is not included in the neighborhood data file.

Heat groups: HVI rank 1–2 = low, 3 = mid, 4–5 = high.

Mental health

PLACES 2025 release; all 2,231 NYC tracts matched to NYC's 2020 tract-to-NTA table, measured 2026-09-26. For each NTA, frequent mental distress (mhlth_crudeprev) and depression (depression_crudeprev) are population-weighted means of the tract values, weighted by PLACES totalpopulation. Every residential NTA has at least two matched tracts; 34 residential tracts in the city's table have no PLACES estimate and are simply absent from the average. Distress groups are tertiles of frequent mental distress across the 197 residential NTAs.

Street trees

Each alive tree in the 2015 census (652,173 trees) is placed in the 2020 NTA polygon that contains it; 85 trees fall outside every NTA polygon. Density is trees divided by NTA land area, computed in the New York State Plane Long Island projection (EPSG:2263) and converted to km².

Statistics

Spearman rank correlations across residential NTAs. The income-adjusted values are partial Spearman correlations: all three variables are rank-transformed, the ranks of each variable in the pair are regressed on the ranks of median household income, and the Pearson correlation of the residuals is reported (p-value from a t-test with n − 2 − k degrees of freedom, where k is the number of control variables). One adjustment also controls for percent Black population, which the HVI uses as an input and which the PLACES models can draw on through individual race/ethnicity.

    Caveats

    • PLACES figures are model-based estimates, not direct survey counts for each neighborhood.
    • PLACES estimates are modeled partly from demographic data, so they share inputs with income-based measures.
    • The tree data is from 2015. Street trees planted or removed since then are not reflected.
    • Street trees are only one kind of greenery; park and private-yard trees are not counted.
    • All figures are correlations across neighborhoods only.
